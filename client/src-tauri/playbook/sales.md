# Sample Sales Call Playbook

This is a **sample** playbook for a made-up company, Acme Cloud. Replace it with your own:
who you are, what you sell, your proof points, objections and trigger words. It is the
coaching context for live hints and goes in the cached part of the prompt, so it can be
detailed without adding per-hint cost.

**[FILL IN]** marks details that need a real answer from your sales team. Hints must not
state anything about them.

---

## 1. Who we are

**Acme Cloud**: a made-up software consultancy that builds and runs cloud and AI products.

- Founded 2015; 40 people
- Offices: Bengaluru, India and Austin, USA
- 24/7 support

## 2. What we sell

### Product Build
A production-ready first version of a product, scoped to the smallest build that proves the most
value. A few weeks, depending on scope. [FILL IN: price range]

### Architecture Review
A short, fixed-scope review of an existing system with a written plan. A good first step for a
cautious buyer.

### Managed Cloud
Running the customer's cloud for them: monitoring, cost tuning, security patches and on-call.

### Dedicated Engineers
Engineers who join the customer's team, billed hourly, with no long-term commitment.

## 3. Proof points

Only reference these at the level of detail given.

| Client | Region | What we built |
|---|---|---|
| Northwind Learning | India | Online course platform for professional exams |
| Contoso Pay | USA | Payments dashboard with real-time reporting |
| Fabrikam Health | Global | Patient scheduling app |

Match the example to the prospect: education → Northwind Learning; fintech → Contoso Pay;
healthcare → Fabrikam Health.

## 4. Call goals: what to find out

Hints should nudge the rep when any of these is still unknown later in the call:

1. **Problem / trigger:** Why now? What's broken, slow, or expensive?
2. **Current setup:** Stack and cloud today? In-house team, or another vendor?
3. **Scale:** Users, data volume, monthly cloud or AI spend
4. **Budget:** Is there a budget, and a rough range?
5. **Timeline:** Deadline, or an event driving it (launch, funding round, renewal)?
6. **Decision process:** Who decides, who else is involved, how do they buy?
7. **Next step:** Always end with a concrete next step with a date

**Suggested discovery questions**
- "What prompted you to look at this now?"
- "Walk me through how it works today. Where does it break?"
- "If this goes well, what does success look like in three months?"
- "Who else needs to be involved in the decision?"
- "Is there a date driving this, like a launch?"

## 5. Buying signals

Hint to move forward (propose a concrete next step with a time) when the prospect:
- Asks about pricing, timelines, team size, or how to get started
- Asks "how would you do X for us?" or goes into technical detail about their system
- Mentions a deadline, a funding round, or a board ask
- Brings in another stakeholder ("let me loop in our CTO")
- Asks about contracts, ownership, or support after launch

## 6. Objections

| Objection | Direction for the hint |
|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | Ask what it's being compared to. Offer the Architecture Review as a smaller fixed-scope first step. [FILL IN: any discounts the rep may offer] |
| "We have an in-house team" | Position as augmentation: specialists who work alongside their engineers. |
| "We already work with another vendor" | Ask what's working and what isn't. Offer the review as a no-commitment second opinion. |
| "Will we own the code?" | Yes: the client owns all code. |
| "What if it doesn't work out?" | No long-term commitment. Start with a fixed-scope review. |
| "Send me an email" / "Let me think about it" | Don't accept a vague brush-off: agree, then lock a specific follow-up time and ask what they need to decide. |
| "Data security / compliance" | [FILL IN: security certifications or practices] |

## 7. Hint style

- One short, actionable line the rep can say or do **right now**
- Prefer a question to ask over a fact to recite
- Only use facts from this playbook; never invent numbers, clients, prices, or guarantees
- Never state anything marked [FILL IN]
- If nothing useful applies, give no hint rather than a generic one

## 8. Vocabulary

Names speech recognition should spell right (sent to the transcription service, up to 50). Not facts for hints.

## 9. Trigger words

When the prospect says one of these, a hint comes at once instead of waiting for a pause. Whole words, any case. Hindi is listed in Roman letters and in Devanagari, since transcripts can use either.

- price, prices, pricing, cost, costs, expensive, budget, discount
- competitor, competitors, contract, timeline, decision
- not interested, think about it, send me an email, already using
- kitna, kitne ka, mehenga, mehnga, mahanga, sasta, discount milega
- baad mein, sochna padega, soch ke batata, soch ke bataenge, interest nahi, zaroorat nahi
- कितना, कितने का, महंगा, महँगा, सस्ता, बजट, डिस्काउंट
- बाद में, सोचना पड़ेगा, सोच के बताता, सोच के बताएंगे, इंटरेस्ट नहीं, ज़रूरत नहीं
